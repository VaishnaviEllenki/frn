The Impact of Food Rescue

Food insecurity and food waste are pressing global issues that impact millions of people worldwide. The United Nations Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) reports that approximately 702–802 million people worldwide faced hunger in 2021, and this number is expected to rise significantly since the COVID-19 pandemic (FAO, 2023).

Meanwhile, the world wastes approximately one-third of the food produced for human consumption, amounting to 1.3 billion tonnes of food waste annually (Gustavsson et al., 2011). To address these concerns, food rescue organisations have emerged as a partial solution, aiming to alleviate short-term food insecurity while simultaneously reducing food waste.

These organisations collect surplus food that would otherwise go to waste from retailers, manufacturers, and other sources and distribute it to those in need. While food rescue is often framed as a ‘win-win’ solution to address both food insecurity and food waste, some have criticised the practice, arguing that it is a temporary fix that does not address the underlying systems and practices that perpetuate both issues. However, others argue that revolutionary change and food redistribution are necessary now. They suggest viewing food rescue as a pragmatic “in-the-meantime” solution to address immediate concerns, while longer-term solutions to food waste and food insecurity are sought.
